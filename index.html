Less chasing. More getting things done.

A useful system starts with a familiar problem. Explore a few ways AI and automation can fit into everyday work.

An order comes in. The follow-up takes care of itself.

Stock checks, order updates and shipping notifications can mean a lot of switching between systems. Connect those steps so your team can focus on the exceptions.

The opportunity
Less re-keying and chasing. A clearer path from order to dispatch.
Where people fit
Your team reviews unusual orders, stock conflicts and anything that needs a judgement call.
Discuss a workflow like this
  1. Receive the orderYour online store
  2. Check stock and order detailsConnected inventory systems
  3. Flag anything unusualA person reviews exceptions
  4. Update systems and notifyOrder records and customer updates

A question arrives. A good reply is ready to check.

Many customer emails ask the same few things. Look up the facts in your systems and draft the reply, so your team spends its time on the conversations that need them.

The opportunity
Faster, more consistent replies, without starting each one from scratch.
Where people fit
Your team approves replies, and handles complaints and anything sensitive.
Discuss a workflow like this
  1. Receive the enquiryEmail or your helpdesk
  2. Find the factsOrders, accounts and policies
  3. Draft a replyIn your tone, with the details filled in
  4. Review and sendA person approves or edits

Someone has a question. The answer is already written down.

Policies, procedures and product details often live in documents nobody has time to search. Let your team ask in plain language and get an answer that points to the source.

The opportunity
Less time hunting for information, and fewer questions for the one person who knows.
Where people fit
Answers show their sources, and say so when the documents don't cover a question.
Discuss a workflow like this
  1. Ask a questionIn plain language
  2. Search your documentsPolicies, guides and records
  3. Answer with sourcesLinked to the page it came from
  4. Check when it mattersA person confirms important answers

Start small. Prove the value. Build from there.

You do not need a fully formed AI strategy to get started. You need a problem worth solving and a clear next step.

  1. Understand your business

    Walk through the work, the tools and the bottlenecks. Agree on the problem and what a useful result would look like.

  2. Choose a focused first project

    Define a manageable scope, the data required, and the checkpoints. Agree on the proposal before the build begins.

  3. Build, test and put it to work

    Develop the solution around your workflow. Test real scenarios, review the results together, and prepare your team to use it.

  4. Review and refine

    See what is working, resolve the rough edges, and decide whether to improve the first workflow or tackle the next one.

Before we get started.

I'm not sure where AI would help. Is that okay?

Yes. Most conversations start there. We walk through how your work gets done, and I'll point out where AI or automation could help, and where it wouldn't be worth it.

Can you work with the tools we already use?

Usually, yes. I build around the systems you already have and connect them where they allow it. If a tool can't be connected, I'll tell you early.

What happens when the AI gets something wrong?

Systems are tested on real scenarios before they go live, and anything uncertain is sent to a person instead of being acted on. You decide which steps always need a human check.

How are projects priced?

After our first conversation, I'll give you a clear proposal with the scope and the price, agreed before any build starts.

Do we need to be in Auckland?

No. I'm based in Auckland and happy to meet in person here, and I work with businesses elsewhere in New Zealand online.

What would you like to make easier?

Tell me about a process that takes too much time, an idea you want to explore, or an AI system that needs a better plan.
